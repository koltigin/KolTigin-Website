Stablecoin Debate in the US, Banks Embrace Digital Assets in Türkiye

KolTigin

X LinkedIn Farcaster

The debate over stablecoins and traditional banking continues in the United States. Meanwhile, regulators are putting new rules in place that could affect how digital assets are used in the financial system.

On October 21, 2026, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) announced its final regulations implementing the GENIUS Act. Published on October 23, the rules are scheduled to take effect on January 18, 2027.[1]

They cover reserve requirements, redemption obligations, custody services, and supervision of stablecoin issuers.

This development brought me back to the CLARITY Act, which I have discussed in previous articles. In particular, I have been thinking about how banks are responding to digital assets and the competition they may bring.

Why Are Banks Concerned About Stablecoins?

One of the main disagreements in the United States concerns the rewards offered to stablecoin holders.

The GENIUS Act prohibits payment stablecoin issuers from paying interest or yield simply for holding their stablecoins. Reward programs offered by third parties, including crypto platforms, remain a separate regulatory issue.

Some US banking groups argue that stablecoin rewards could encourage customers to move money out of traditional deposit accounts.

I can understand their concern. Deposits are an important source of funding for bank lending. If customers begin moving substantial amounts of money into other financial products, banks could face changes in their funding structure.

But I think there is another question worth asking.

Should banks try to limit competition from new financial technologies, or should they use those technologies to improve their own services?

Turkish Banks Are Preparing for Digital Assets

When I look at developments in Türkiye, I see traditional financial institutions taking an increasingly active interest in crypto assets.

Garanti BBVA offers crypto asset services through Garanti BBVA Kripto. Customers can also view their crypto portfolios within the main Garanti BBVA mobile banking application.[2]

In 2023, Ak Yatırım acquired a majority stake in Stablex, marking its entry into the crypto asset sector.[3]

Yapı Kredi has also taken steps toward establishing a crypto asset platform. In March 2026, Türkiye's Capital Markets Board (SPK) approved its company establishment application.[4]

These are not isolated examples. Akbank, Garanti BBVA, Türkiye İş Bankası, and Yapı Kredi have also appeared on the SPK's lists in connection with applications for crypto asset custody services.[5]

An application or approval to establish a company does not necessarily mean that every required operating license has been granted.

Still, I find these developments significant.

A few years ago, crypto assets were often viewed as an alternative market operating outside traditional banking. Today, established financial institutions are investing in that market and developing services for it.

Of course, offering crypto trading services is not the same as operating a stablecoin or tokenized deposit system. The regulatory environments in Türkiye and the United States are also quite different.

Even so, I see these initiatives as signs that Turkish banks are preparing for a financial system in which digital assets may play a larger role.

Why Does the CLARITY Act Still Matter?

While the United States is implementing regulations under the GENIUS Act, the CLARITY Act has not yet become law.

The CLARITY Act aims to establish a broader regulatory framework for digital asset markets and clarify the division of responsibilities between the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC).

On September 15, 2026, the Senate failed to reach the 60 votes required to advance the bill. The procedural vote ended with 49 votes in favor and 50 against, leaving the legislation stalled.[6]

That does not mean the need for regulatory clarity has disappeared.

I believe clearer rules would be particularly important for tokenization, digital asset custody, and institutional blockchain applications.

Companies developing these technologies need to understand which rules apply to their activities. Uncertainty makes long-term planning more difficult.

How I See the Future of Banking

I expect stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and the tokenization of real-world assets to receive growing attention from financial institutions in the coming years.

These technologies have different legal and technical characteristics. What they share is the possibility of changing how money and financial assets are represented, transferred, and managed.

I welcome the steps Turkish banks are taking in this area.

Protecting financial stability, consumers, and depositors remains essential. I do not see those responsibilities as incompatible with developing new financial services.

My view is that banks should not treat digital assets solely as a threat to their existing business models.

One of the factors shaping the future of banking will be how well banks adapt to new technologies, rather than how effectively they can resist change.
